Product Marketing

Teaching the rules through play, not a manual.

From a product marketing perspective, it is more useful for a customer to see a game in action than to read a page of instructions. A short ten second play helps someone register the rules far faster than text does, and it is the first impression most people will actually have time for. Below is a demo I built of what that could look like: a full, working round of UNO against the computer. Watch the glowing cards and the message line, they are doing the explaining.

Where this idea came from

In almost every group I'm in, there's usually one person who reads through a new game and ends up teaching it to everyone else, and I have noticed that person is often me. That puts pressure on one person to learn the rules fast and explain them well, while everyone who would rather not read a rules card just sits there waiting, and that friction can stop a game before it starts. The clearest example was a group trying to learn Dos. The rules felt confusing enough on the page that we almost did not play at all. While I was reading through them out loud and explaining each one, it occurred to me that a short interactive walkthrough would have solved the whole problem before it ever came up. That is where the guided example below came from.

Industrial Engineering

Packaging efficiency, worked out live.

From a sustainability and packaging perspective, I think about efficiency and lower cost first, then about what happens after the box is opened. The last stage of a product's life cycle matters too: packaging that can be recycled easily, or reused as a storage container once the game is out of the box, is worth designing for from the start rather than bolting on later. Redesign concepts: Hot Wheels packaging

The same engineering lens applied to a different Mattel line. Three directions, each solving a different problem with the current blister pack, followed by a set of CAD style concept drawings for each. The window box concept is drawn with its end of life in mind too, it is built to double as a small storage container once the car is out, which is the kind of thinking behind programs like Mattel PlayBack, Mattel's own toy take back and recycling program.

Eco friendly materials

  • Replace single use plastic blisters with biodegradable or plant based plastic where a window is still needed.
  • Move to fully recyclable, plastic free cardboard boxes with a window or a protective sleeve instead of a bubble.

Collector focused durability

  • Use heavier, rigid cardstock so the backing card does not bend, crease, or warp in shipping or on a shelf.
  • Strengthen the seal between the plastic bubble and the card so it does not detach on its own.

Reusability and display

  • A tuck flap or sliding tray lets a collector open, store, and put the car back without destroying the card art.
  • A built in stand or foldable easel back lets an unopened card stand upright on a shelf.
